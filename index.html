Understanding Trichology

What Is Trichology?

Trichology is the study of the hair and scalp, and of the factors that affect their health. A trichologist looks closely at your scalp, your hair, and your history to understand why changes like thinning, shedding, or irritation may be happening.

Trichology is not a replacement for medical care. When something points to an underlying health concern, I refer you to a physician and work alongside them.

From the Greek trikhos, meaning hair

  3. 03

  4. 04

    Refer

    When your concern needs medical evaluation, I refer you to a physician and continue supporting your care alongside them.

Healthy Scalp Habits

Scalp Care Do's & Don'ts

Small daily habits make a real difference in keeping your scalp healthy and helping prevent hair loss, for every hair type.

Do

  • Do: Cleanse your scalp regularly to clear oil, sweat, and product buildup.
  • Do: Eat a balanced diet with enough protein, iron, and other nutrients that support healthy hair growth.
  • Do: Detangle gently with a wide-tooth comb or your fingers, starting from the ends.
  • Do: Give your hair regular breaks from tight styles, extensions, and heavy wigs.
  • Do: Protect your scalp from the sun, especially along your part.
  • Do: Get sudden shedding, patches, or scalp changes checked early, when they are easiest to address.

Don't

  • Don't: Wear tight braids, ponytails, or extensions for long stretches without a break.
  • Don't: Scratch or pick at an itchy, flaky, or irritated scalp.
  • Don't: Overuse heat styling, bleaching, or chemical treatments.
  • Don't: Let products sit on your scalp for days without cleansing.
  • Don't: Crash diet. Rapid weight loss is a common trigger for shedding a few months later.
  • Don't: Ignore bald patches, scarring, or burning on the scalp. Some types of hair loss are permanent if left untreated.

Every scalp is different. A consultation turns these basics into a plan built for you.

Myths vs Facts

Clearing Up Common Misconceptions

Much of what people hear about hair loss is outdated or oversimplified. Here is what the evidence and clinical experience actually suggest.

  • Myth

    Washing your hair often causes hair loss.

    Fact

    The hairs you see in the shower were usually already in their resting phase and ready to shed. Washing simply rinses them away. Shampooing at a frequency that suits your scalp helps keep it clean and balanced, which supports healthier growth.

  • Myth

    Wearing hats causes baldness.

    Fact

    An ordinary, clean hat does not cause pattern hair loss, which is driven mainly by genetics and hormones. Very tight headwear worn constantly can add tension, so comfort and a clean fit still matter.

  • Myth

    Hair transplant results are immediate.

    Fact

    Transplanted hairs commonly shed in the first weeks, and new growth tends to appear gradually over many months. Results develop over roughly a year or longer, and every person's timeline is different.

  • Myth

    Hair loss is only a men's issue.

    Fact

    Many women experience thinning, often linked to hormones, postpartum changes, stress, nutrition, or genetics. It can look different from male pattern hair loss, which is why a careful assessment matters.

  • Myth

    Scalp care doesn't matter after a transplant.

    Fact

    Gentle, consistent aftercare is an important part of healing. Following your surgeon's instructions and caring for your scalp helps protect new grafts, and ongoing scalp health supports your existing hair as well.

FAQs

Frequently Asked Questions

Answers to the questions we hear most, from what to expect at your first visit to how hair transplant support works.

What happens during my first consultation?

Your first consultation begins with a detailed intake covering your medical history, hair care routine, genetics, stress factors, and nutritional habits. Following that conversation, you'll receive a personalized written treatment plan with recommendations tailored to your specific hair and scalp.

Do I need to come in person, or is this all virtual?

Consultations and scalp assessments are conducted virtually. For the scalp analysis, you'll submit high-resolution photos from prescribed angles, which are used to evaluate follicle health, scalp condition, and density.

I'm considering a hair transplant. Where do I start?

Start with the Pre & Post Hair Transplant Consultation. The pre-transplant portion assesses your candidacy, helps optimize your scalp health before surgery, and sets realistic expectations. If you haven't chosen a surgeon yet, the Hair Transplant Referrals service can connect you with a vetted provider matched to your needs.

What's the difference between FUE and FUT?

FUE (Follicular Unit Extraction) and FUT (Follicular Unit Transplantation) are the two primary hair transplant techniques, and the right choice depends on your goals, donor area, and the extent of your hair loss. This is covered in detail as part of the Hair Transplant Referrals service, alongside guidance on evaluating surgeons.

How soon will I see results after a hair transplant?

Transplanted follicles typically go through a shedding and dormant phase before new growth becomes visible, so results take months to fully develop. Structured post-transplant care is designed to protect newly transplanted follicles and support that long-term growth and retention.

What is The Hair Restoration Roadmap?

It is a structured educational program covering scalp health fundamentals, nutrition for hair growth, stress and hormonal influences, and proper at-home care, delivered through video lessons and downloadable guides. It is designed to help you understand the root cause of your hair loss, not just how to treat it. You can explore it on the Learn page.

Do you treat postpartum hair loss?

Yes. Hormonal shifts after pregnancy can trigger noticeable shedding, and a scalp assessment or consultation is a good starting point to understand what's happening and support your scalp through recovery.

I have natural or textured hair. Does that change my care plan?

Yes, textured and natural hair have unique needs, including traction-related thinning and styling-related stress, and your treatment plan is built around your specific hair type from the start.

What photos do I need for a virtual scalp analysis?

You send clear, high-resolution pictures of your scalp from the angles I request, in good natural light. I will tell you exactly which views to take when you book.

Do you offer laser hair therapy?

Yes. I offer the Z Cap, a fixed, free-standing low-level laser for hair that you use at home. Its panels sit around your scalp on an adjustable stand, so sessions are hands-free. We start with a consultation to see if it fits your type of hair loss, and I guide you on using it with the rest of your plan. Results take time, typically at least a few months of consistent use. Learn more about laser therapy.

Do you sell hair care products directly?

I recommend professional products that support your treatment plan. Orders ship directly to you from trusted suppliers.

Do you offer free presentations?

Yes. I share free presentations on hair loss, hair loss prevention, and different scalp issues. You can watch them on the Learn page and on my Facebook page.
